🪨 Part 1Fossils Are Clues in Stone

A fossil is what is left of a living thing from long, long ago. Bones, shells, leaves, and even footprints can become fossils.

Fossils hold clues, like the shape of a fin or the lines in a leaf. Each clue is a piece of evidence.

🐟 Part 2Fossils Tell Us About Ancient Living Things

Scientists look closely at a fossil's shape. A fish fossil with fins and a backbone shows what the fish looked like and how it swam. A tooth's shape tells what an animal ate. When scientists use clues like these to figure something out, they infer.

Many of these living things are extinct. None are alive anywhere on Earth today. Fossils are the only way we know they lived.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Gone forever
This is a trilobite. Trilobites lived in the sea for about 270 million years. Then every last one died out. They are extinct, so fossils are the only way we know they lived.

🌊 Part 3Fossils Tell Us About Ancient Places

Here is a surprise: scientists have found sea shells on mountaintops and fish in dry deserts. That is not a mistake. It is evidence that those places were very different long ago.

A fossil tells you about its environment. A fern fossil means the land was warm and wet. A coral fossil means warm, shallow sea water once covered that spot.

🎬 Director's cut

The top of the world was once the sea floor

The rock at the very top of Mount Everest is limestone, a rock that forms in the sea. Scientists have found fossils of sea animals in it, including trilobites.

Very slowly, over millions of years, that old sea floor was pushed up into the sky.

⭐ Key idea: Fossils are evidence. They show what living things looked like and where they lived, even if those living things are extinct and those places have changed. In undisturbed rock, deeper layers hold older fossils.
