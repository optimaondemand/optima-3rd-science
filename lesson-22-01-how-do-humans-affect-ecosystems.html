🌱 Part 1Humans Use Ecosystem Resources

Every day, people use things that come from ecosystems: water, wood, soil, food, and land. We need these resources to live, just like other animals do.

When people use resources, they often change the habitat for plants and animals that already live there. Even a small change, like a new path through the woods or a parking lot next to a creek, can change what living things can find.

⚠️ Part 2When Human Actions Harm

Some human actions make it harder for living things to survive. Trash and pollution dirty the water. Cutting down forests takes away shelter. Paving land takes away space, and rain runs off the pavement instead of soaking in.

The impact doesn't have to be huge to matter. One pond full of litter can drive away fish, frogs, and the birds that eat them.

🎬 Behind the scenes · A garden that drinks the rain
This rain garden in Singapore sits next to a path. Rain from the pavement flows into it and soaks into the soil and roots instead of rushing to a drain. Trees help too: the USDA Forest Service says a medium-sized tree can catch up to about 2,400 gallons of rain in a year.

💚 Part 3When Human Actions Help

People can also protect and restore ecosystems. Planting native wildflowers gives bees food. Picking up trash clears harmful things from water and soil. This work is called conservation.

A solution that only looks nice isn't really working. It has to give back food, water, shelter, or space. In the Florida Everglades, people are bringing back fresh water that canals blocked, and wading birds are starting to return to some areas.

🎬 Director's cut

The pink birds that need shallow water

Roseate spoonbills sweep their spoon-shaped bills side to side through shallow water to catch small fish and shrimp. When canals drained the Everglades, they lost many feeding spots.

Now Florida and the U.S. government are working on one of the biggest wetland restorations ever, sending fresh water south again. Scientists count the birds each year to see if the plan is working.

⭐ Key idea: Human actions can harm or help ecosystems by changing the resources organisms need: food, water, shelter, and space. When resources disappear, organisms struggle to survive. When resources are restored, organisms can return and thrive.
