🌱 Part 1Plants Can't Walk, But They Can Grow

When light shines from one side, a plant can't pick up its roots and walk toward it. But it can slowly grow in that direction.

This is called phototropism. "Photo" means light. The stem and leaves bend toward the light. It happens over hours and days, too slowly to see while you watch.

🔦 Part 2Light Is the Stimulus

A stimulus is something in the environment that causes a reaction. For a plant near a window, light is the stimulus.

The plant's response is to bend toward the light. The plant doesn't decide to do this. Its stem grows a little longer on the shady side, so the stem bends toward the bright side.

Roots are different. They grow down into the soil, because they respond to gravity, not light.

🎬 Behind the scenes · A fair test
Real plant scientists grow seedlings in greenhouses and growth rooms, where they can control the light. In a fair test, they change just one thing, like where the light comes from, and keep everything else the same.

📈 Part 3Evidence in the Data

Scientists observe a plant over several days to find its growth pattern. One day of data doesn't show much. Four or five days together can show a clear direction.

When the stem bends more each day, always toward the same side, that's evidence. Farmers and gardeners use this. They put plants where they'll get the most sunlight.

🎬 Director's cut

Darwin's tiny caps

Darwin and his son Francis wondered which part of a seedling notices the light. So they made tiny caps out of tin foil and thin glass tubes painted black. They slipped the caps over the tips of grass seedlings.

When the tip was covered, the seedling hardly bent at all, even though the rest of it was in the light! The tip notices the light, and the bending happens lower down.

⭐ Key idea: Phototropism is a plant's growth response to light. Light is the stimulus, and the bending stem and turning leaves are the response. Scientists find this growth pattern by observing the same plant over several days.
