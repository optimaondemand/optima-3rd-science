♻️ Part 1Reducing Waste

Every day, people throw things away: packaging, paper, bottles. A lot of it doesn't have to be waste. Recycling turns old materials into new ones. Aluminum cans get melted and made into new cans.

Even smarter is to reduce: buy less, choose less packaging, and reuse things first. Less trash going in means less harm coming out.

💧 Part 2Water Conservation

Most of Earth's water is salty. Only a small part is fresh water we can drink or use to grow food. Water conservation means making careful choices: turning off the tap while brushing your teeth, fixing leaks, and covering garden soil with mulch so it stays damp. A faucet that drips once every second can waste more than 3,000 gallons in a year.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Florida-friendly yards
Florida's gardening experts at the University of Florida say to keep 2 to 3 inches of mulch on plant beds. It holds water in the soil and keeps weeds from sprouting. Lots of Florida families also pick native plants, which need less watering once they're growing.

🌿 Part 3Protecting Habitats

When land is cleared for buildings or roads, animals lose their homes. Communities protect habitats by setting aside nature preserves, planting native plants, and keeping streams and wetlands clean.

Not every action works the same way. The best choice depends on the problem you're trying to solve.

Buried isn't gone. A landfill keeps trash out of sight, but much of it lasts for many years. That's why reducing and recycling come first: less trash means smaller landfills.

Think like a scientist

A friend says, "Throwing away one cup a day doesn't matter." What does the evidence say?

🎬 Director's cut

From the world's biggest landfill to a giant park

For more than 50 years, Fresh Kills was one of the biggest landfills in the world. Barges brought New York City's trash here every day. It closed in 2001.

Now workers are covering the trash hills with liners and soil and planting grass. It's becoming Freshkills Park, about three times the size of Central Park. Hawks, foxes, and grassland birds already live there.

⭐ Key idea: People can take real steps to reduce their impact on the environment. Reducing waste, conserving water, and protecting habitats all make a difference, but only if people actually do them. Reducing comes before recycling, and small actions add up when many people do them.
