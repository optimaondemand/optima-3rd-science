Scene 2The Lesson

OptiThree scenarios, three investigations: a burned forest, a bright beach, and a mountain that's getting warmer.

🧠 Part 1You Already Think Like a Scientist

Remember the old pages? They come from the field book of Vernon Bailey, a scientist who studied animals. In 1890 in Nevada, he drew the tracks of a kind of kangaroo rat. It hops on two big back feet, so its prints come in pairs. He wrote the date, too. That's what scientists do: observe, record, and use evidence.

Scenario A · A Mountain Park. In 1988, fires burned about a third of Yellowstone National Park. What grows back?

🏖 Part 2Scenario B · A New Parking Lot

Sea turtles nest on Florida beaches every summer. At night, the hatchlings dig out and crawl toward the brightest light. On a natural beach, that's the moon and stars over the ocean. Now a new parking lot with bright lights is being built near the beach. What will the hatchlings do?

🎬 Behind the scenes · Counting hatchlings
Every nesting season, trained teams walk Florida beaches at sunrise. They mark nests, count tracks, and check which way the hatchlings went. That evidence shows towns where their lights need to change.

🏔 Part 3Scenario C · Shorter, Warmer Winters

On a mountain, it gets colder the higher you go. Each plant and animal lives where the temperature fits it. In parks like Rocky Mountain National Park in Colorado, winters are getting shorter and warmer. Scientists check the same places year after year, looking for a pattern.

🏔 The Mountain ClimbExplore a mountain, one stop at a time

👀 What am I looking at? A model of a mountain in Rocky Mountain National Park, cut into four stops from the top down. Each stop holds a clue and a real photo. The dial on the left shows how high up you are, in feet.

What to do: First, make a prediction. Then brush the glowing stop: drag across it (or tap it again and again) until the photo shows.

Living things live where the conditions fit them. When winters get shorter and warmer, snow melts sooner, flowers bloom earlier, and animals that need cold, like pikas, can only live higher up. In some mountains, scientists now find pikas only higher up than where they lived long ago.

🎬 Director's cut

Old Faithful Kept Erupting

On September 7, 1988, fire swept right up to the buildings at Old Faithful. Firefighters worked to save them, and the geyser kept erupting, just as it always had.

The next summer, scientists walking the burned ground found tiny pine seedlings almost everywhere they looked. The forest was already starting over.

⭐ Key idea: Scientists ask a question, make a prediction, collect evidence, look for patterns, and explain what they found. That's how you studied a burned forest, a bright beach, and a warming mountain today, and it's how you studied weather, forces, life cycles, plants, and ecosystems all year. Evidence is what makes a conclusion stronger than a guess.
