🌱 Part 1Plants Need to Make New Plants

When a plant reproduces, it makes new plants just like itself. Without reproduction, plants would eventually disappear.

Some plants reproduce using seeds. Others, like ferns and mosses, reproduce using spores. Both are ways of starting a new life.

🐝 Part 2Flowers, Pollen, and Pollinators

Many plants grow flowers. Flowers make a powdery material called pollen. For seeds to form, pollen has to travel from one flower to another. That is pollination.

Wind can carry pollen, but animals do most of the work. Bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds are pollinators. They pick up pollen while they feed, then drop it off at the next flower.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Seeds don't just drop
This coconut floated in the sea, washed up between rocks, and sprouted a new palm. If every seed dropped right under its parent, the young plants would crowd together and fight for light and water. Seeds that travel get room to grow.

🌍 Part 3Seeds, Spores, and Getting Around

After pollination, many plants form seeds. Seeds need to get away from the parent plant to find space to grow. They travel by wind, water, animals, and even gravity.

Ferns don't make flowers or seeds at all. They release tiny spores from the undersides of their leaves. The spores drift through the air, and some grow into new ferns.

🎬 Director's cut

Seeds that sail the sea

Sea hearts grow on giant vines in tropical forests. Their pods can grow longer than you are tall! Each seed has a hollow space inside, so it floats.

When the seeds fall into rivers, they wash out to sea. Some ride the Gulf Stream for a year or more and land on beaches in Florida, and even Scotland and Norway. Long ago, people in Scotland found them and called them Molucca beans.

⭐ Key idea: Plants reproduce: they make new plants of the same kind. Flowering plants often use pollination and seeds. Ferns and mosses use spores instead. Either way, the goal is the same: new life.
