💧 Part 1What Does Pollution Do to Water?

Pollution gets into rivers, lakes, and the ocean from oil spills, trash, and chemicals that wash off farms. Once it mixes in, the water is contaminated. Animals that live in it or drink it can get sick, and plants can die.

Most oil is lighter than water, so it floats on top and spreads out in a thin, shiny layer.

🧹 Part 2Three Ways to Clean Up Pollution

Engineers test different cleanup methods to find out which works best.

🧻 Absorb: a pad or sponge soaks up floating oil. 🔽 Filter: water passes through something that traps the pollution. 🥄 Skim: a net, spoon, or boat scoops floating stuff off the top.

🧪 Try the real cup test (with an adult)

Fill 3 clear cups halfway with water and stir 1 teaspoon of cooking oil into each. Cup 1: lay a paper towel on top for 30 seconds (absorb). Cup 2: pour it through a coffee filter (filter). Cup 3: scoop with a spoon for 60 seconds (skim). Rate each one 1, 2, or 3.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Miles of boom
In April 2010, workers loaded floating booms onto boats at Pensacola, Florida. By the day the leak was stopped, crews had laid out more than 600 miles of boom to hold oil back, plus even more absorbent boom to soak it up. Rescuers also washed hundreds of oiled birds and let them go.

⭐ Part 3Why Choosing the Right Method Matters

Not every method works the same way. A net can't catch a thin film of oil, and a pad can't pick up a floating bottle. Some pollution, like fertilizer, dissolves (mixes in so you can't see it), so it has to be filtered out.

After the 2010 Gulf oil spill, crews used booms, skimmers, and absorbent pads side by side, picking the right tool for each place.

🎬 Director's cut

Oysters: living filters

One grown-up oyster can filter up to about 50 gallons of water a day as it pulls in water to eat tiny bits of food. A whole reef of oysters cleans a lot of water.

That's why people in Florida, from Tampa Bay to the Indian River Lagoon, are building new oyster reefs. It's a cleanup method that is alive.

⭐ Key idea: Pollution in water can often be cleaned up, but no single method works for everything. Absorbing works on thin oil, skimming works on floating trash and oil held by a boom, and filtering works on pollution mixed into the water. Engineers test and compare methods to find the most effective one, and even then some pollution can stay behind.
