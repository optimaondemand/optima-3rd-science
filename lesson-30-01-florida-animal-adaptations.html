🌱 Part 1What Is an Adaptation?

An adaptation is a body part or behavior that helps an animal survive in its habitat. Adaptations are traits passed down from parents. An animal can't grow a new one just because it needs it.

Animals also respond when the seasons change. In winter, manatees swim to warm springs, and many birds migrate south to Florida from colder places.

🌤 Part 2Florida Animals Up Close

An alligator's eyes and nostrils sit on top of its head, so it can hide underwater and still see and breathe. A Florida panther's padded paws let it walk quietly, and its tan fur blends with the forest.

Camouflage helps predators sneak up on prey. It also helps prey hide.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Wet wings
An anhinga's feathers soak up water, so it can sink and swim underwater to spear fish. After a dive, it spreads its wings in the sun to dry them before it can fly well.

🌍 Part 3Animal Groups

Scientists sort animals into groups by their body features. Mammals have fur and feed their babies milk. Birds have feathers. Reptiles have dry scales. Amphibians have smooth, wet skin. Fish have scales and breathe with gills.

All of these are vertebrates: animals with a backbone. Insects and spiders have no backbone. Some animals, like panthers, give birth to live young; others, like alligators, lay eggs.

🎬 Director's cut

Only in Florida

The Florida scrub-jay lives nowhere else in the world. It lives only in Florida's dry, sandy scrub.

Each fall, a scrub-jay buries thousands of acorns in the sand, then digs many of them up to eat later.

⭐ Key idea: Adaptations are traits that help animals survive in their habitats, and they are passed down from parents. Florida animals have adaptations that match their homes: an alligator's eyes on top of its head, a panther's quiet paws, a lizard's sandy camouflage. Animals also respond to the seasons, like manatees swimming to warm springs in winter. Scientists sort animals into groups, like mammals, birds, reptiles, and amphibians, by their body features.
