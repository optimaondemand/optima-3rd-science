🕸 Part 1Every Living Thing Has Neighbors

Remember the mystery specimen? It's an owl pellet: the fur, bones, and beetle shells an owl couldn't digest. One small lump holds pieces of a food web.

Think about a Florida wetland. Fish eat tiny water plants. A heron eats the fish. An alligator may eat the heron. If the pond dries up, the fish disappear, and so does the heron. That's interdependence. Scientists call the whole connected community, living and nonliving, an ecosystem.

🌦 Part 2Weather Writes the Rules

Climate decides which plants can grow in a place, and plants decide which animals can live there. Florida's wet summers flood the sawgrass, and the water brings food and shelter for many birds. In the Everglades, rain sets the water level, and so do canals that people built.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Measuring the marsh
Scientists visit many spots across the Everglades to measure and test the water. They share what they find, so the people who manage the water can decide where it should flow.

🌍 Part 3Traits, Change, and Human Hands

Living things have adaptations that fit their home. A spoonbill sweeps its flat bill through shallow water to catch small fish. Mangrove roots hold tight in soft, flooded mud. But places can change faster than living things can adapt. People drain wetlands and cut forests. People can also choose to protect places, like Everglades National Park.

🎬 Director's cut

The Gator's Water Hole

Alligators use their snouts, feet, and tails to clear mud and plants out of low spots. The cleared hole fills with water. In the dry season, it can be some of the only water around.

Fish, turtles, snails, and birds crowd in. The alligator eats some of them, but many more survive until the rain comes back.

⭐ Key idea: Everything in nature is connected. Weather shapes which plants grow. Plants shape which animals survive. Animals shape the soil and water. A change in one part causes a chain of changes through the rest. This is a system: a set of parts that depend on each other. People can harm any part of the system, and people can choose to protect it.
