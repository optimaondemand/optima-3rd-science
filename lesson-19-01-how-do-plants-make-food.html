☀️ Part 1Plants Make Their Own Food

Animals eat food to get energy. Plants do something different: they make their own food. The process is called photosynthesis.

A plant captures sunlight and uses it to turn water and air into sugar. That sugar is the plant's food. It gives the plant energy to grow new roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds.

🍃 Part 2Leaves, Roots, and Air Work Together

Three things go into photosynthesis: sunlight, water, and air. Leaves face the sun and soak up light. Tiny openings in leaves let air in. The plant uses one gas from the air, called carbon dioxide.

Roots pull water up from the soil. The water travels up the stem to the leaves. Inside each leaf, the plant combines sunlight, water, and air to make sugar. The sugar travels through the stem to every part that needs energy.

🎬 Behind the scenes · Dark versus light
Scientists grew two tiny seedlings of the same kind of plant. One grew in the dark, and one grew in the light. The dark one stretched tall and thin, with no green leaves. The one in the light opened green leaves and started making food.

🔬 Part 3How We Know Sunlight Matters

Scientists figured this out by taking sunlight away. A plant kept in the dark stops making food. Its leaves turn yellow, and it gets smaller and weaker.

A plant in bright light stays green and keeps making sugar. Same plant, same water: the only difference is light. That difference is the evidence that sunlight is the key ingredient.

🎬 Director's cut

A tree that breathes with you

Every leaf on a tree is covered with tiny openings. Air goes in, and the tree keeps one gas from it: carbon dioxide.

Scientists estimate a grown tree takes in about 48 pounds of carbon dioxide each year. As it makes sugar, it lets out oxygen, the gas you breathe!

⭐ Key idea: Plants make their own food through photosynthesis. They use sunlight, water, and air to make sugar, which gives them energy to grow. Leaves capture light. Roots absorb water.
